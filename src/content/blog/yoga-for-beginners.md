You don't need to be flexible to start yoga. That's a bit like saying you need to be clean to take a shower. Yoga is how you build flexibility, along with balance, strength and a calmer mind.

This routine takes about 15 minutes and uses six beginner-friendly poses. All you need is a mat or a rug and some comfortable clothes.

## Before you begin

- **Go slowly.** Move into each pose gradually, and breathe steadily through your nose.
- **Mild stretch, not pain.** You should feel a gentle pull. Sharp, pinching or joint pain means ease off.
- **Use support.** A cushion, folded blanket or chair makes poses more comfortable. That's not cheating; teachers use props all the time.
- **Check first** if you're pregnant, have recently had surgery, or have back, joint or blood pressure problems.

## The routine

### 1. Easy seated breathing (2 minutes)

Sit cross-legged on a cushion, or on a chair with your feet flat. Lengthen your spine and rest your hands on your knees. Breathe in for four counts and out for six. This settles your mind and shifts your attention to your body. Our guide to [breathing exercises for stress](/blog/breathing-exercises-for-stress/) has more techniques.

### 2. Cat-cow (2 minutes)

Come onto your hands and knees, wrists under shoulders, knees under hips. As you breathe in, let your belly drop and lift your chest and gaze (cow). As you breathe out, round your back towards the ceiling and tuck your chin (cat). Move slowly with your breath. This gently mobilises the whole spine.

### 3. Downward-facing dog (2 minutes)

From hands and knees, tuck your toes and lift your hips up and back, making an upside-down V. Keep your knees bent as much as you need; the aim is a long back, not straight legs. Hold for five slow breaths, rest in child's pose, then repeat.

### 4. Low lunge (3 minutes)

Step your right foot forward between your hands and lower your left knee to the mat, with padding under it. Lift your chest and rest your hands on your front thigh. You'll feel a stretch at the front of the left hip, an area that gets tight from sitting. Hold for five to eight breaths, then switch sides.

### 5. Child's pose (2 minutes)

Kneel, bring your big toes together and sit back towards your heels, then fold forward and rest your forehead on the mat or a cushion. Arms can stretch forward or rest by your sides. This is a resting pose you can return to at any time.

### 6. Legs up the wall (4 minutes)

Sit sideways next to a wall, then swing your legs up as you lie back, so your legs rest against the wall and your back is on the floor. Shuffle closer or further away until it's comfortable. Close your eyes and breathe. This is a lovely pose to end with, especially in the evening as part of a [bedtime wind-down](/blog/how-to-fall-asleep-faster/).

## How often should you practise?

Three sessions a week is a realistic and effective start. Many people notice they feel looser and calmer within two to three weeks. Once the routine feels easy, add a few minutes or try a beginners' class, where a teacher can check your alignment.

## Is yoga enough exercise?

Gentle yoga counts as a muscle-strengthening and balance activity, which the NHS recommends on at least two days a week. It doesn't replace aerobic activity, so pair it with something that raises your heart rate, such as brisk walking.

## The bottom line

Start small, move slowly and be patient with your body. Fifteen minutes of gentle yoga a few times a week is enough to feel a real difference in how you move and how you feel.
