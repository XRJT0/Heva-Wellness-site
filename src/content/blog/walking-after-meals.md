Of all the health habits you could add to your day, a short walk after eating is one of the easiest. It needs no equipment, no gym, and very little time. And the research behind it is surprisingly encouraging.

## What happens after you eat

When you eat a meal containing carbohydrates, your blood sugar (glucose) rises over the following hour or so. Your body releases insulin to move that glucose into your cells. For most healthy people this works smoothly, but large or frequent spikes, especially alongside long periods of sitting, are linked over time with a higher risk of type 2 diabetes and heart disease.

## How walking helps

Working muscles take up glucose from the blood, and they can do some of this without needing as much insulin. So when you move gently after a meal, your muscles act like a sponge, soaking up some of that rising glucose.

Studies that compared sitting after a meal with standing or walking have generally found:

- **Light walking lowered the post-meal glucose rise** more than sitting, and usually more than standing alone.
- **Short bouts count.** Even a few minutes of walking showed benefits in some studies, and around 10–15 minutes appears to be a practical sweet spot.
- **Timing matters.** Starting within about 30 minutes of finishing a meal, when glucose is rising, seems most effective.

Many of these studies were small and short-term, so they tell us about immediate effects rather than long-term health outcomes. But the habit is low-risk and also adds up towards your weekly activity.

## Other benefits

- **Digestion:** gentle movement can ease feelings of fullness and bloating for some people.
- **Activity totals:** three 10-minute walks a day, five days a week, add up to the 150 minutes of moderate activity the NHS recommends for adults.
- **Mood and sleep:** daylight and movement both support a healthier body clock. See our guide to [falling asleep faster](/blog/how-to-fall-asleep-faster/).

## How to make it stick

The best walk is the one you actually take. A few ways to build it in:

1. **Attach it to one meal first.** Dinner is often easiest. Aim for 10 minutes, five evenings a week.
2. **Make it social.** Walk with a partner, child, friend or dog, or call someone while you walk.
3. **Have an indoor plan.** On wet days, walk around the house, climb the stairs a few times, or do light chores like tidying the kitchen.
4. **Keep the pace easy.** You should be able to talk comfortably. This isn't a workout.
5. **Track the habit, not the steps.** A simple tick on a calendar is often more motivating than chasing a number.

## A note for people with diabetes

If you have diabetes and take insulin or medicines that can cause low blood sugar (such as sulfonylureas), activity can affect your levels. Speak to your diabetes team about how and when to walk, and keep your usual hypo treatment with you.

## The bottom line

A short, easy walk after meals is one of the simplest changes you can make. It may help steady blood sugar, it counts towards your weekly activity, and it's a pleasant way to end a meal. Start with one meal a day and build from there.
