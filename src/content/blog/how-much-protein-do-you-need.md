Protein is having a moment. It's added to cereal bars, yoghurts and even crisps. But how much do you actually need, and is it worth paying extra for "high protein" products?

## Why protein matters

Protein provides the building blocks (amino acids) your body uses to build and repair muscle, skin, hair and enzymes. It also helps you feel full, which is one reason higher-protein meals can be useful when you're trying to manage your weight.

## How much do you need?

In the UK, the reference intake for healthy adults is about **0.75g of protein per kilogram of body weight per day**. For example:

| Body weight | Daily protein (0.75g/kg) |
| --- | --- |
| 55kg | about 41g |
| 70kg | about 53g |
| 85kg | about 64g |

This is the amount that meets the needs of almost all healthy adults. Some groups benefit from more:

- **Adults over 65:** muscle is harder to maintain with age, and many experts suggest around **1.0–1.2g per kg**.
- **Regular strength or endurance training:** intakes around **1.2–1.6g per kg** are commonly used.
- **During weight loss:** a slightly higher intake can help preserve muscle while you lose fat.

People with kidney disease may need to limit protein, so check with your doctor or dietitian.

## Spread it across the day

Your body uses protein best when it arrives in regular doses. Many people eat little at breakfast and most of their protein at dinner. A more even split, roughly **20–30g per meal**, supports muscle maintenance more effectively.

## What 20–30g looks like

- 3 eggs plus a slice of wholemeal toast
- 200g Greek yoghurt with a handful of nuts
- A palm-sized chicken breast or salmon fillet
- 150g firm tofu
- A tin of chickpeas or lentils (drained), shared between two, plus a sprinkle of seeds
- 2 slices of wholemeal bread with 100g cottage cheese

## Plant-based? You can still get plenty

Beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu, tempeh, edamame, nuts, seeds and whole grains all provide protein. Combining different plant sources across the day gives you the full range of amino acids. You don't need to combine them in the same meal.

## Do you need protein shakes?

For most people, no. Food provides protein alongside fibre, vitamins and minerals. Shakes can be convenient if you have a high requirement, a small appetite, or limited time, but they're a supplement to a balanced diet, not a requirement.

## A simple day that hits around 80g

- **Breakfast:** Greek yoghurt, berries and a tablespoon of mixed seeds (about 20g)
- **Lunch:** lentil and vegetable soup with a wholemeal roll and a small piece of cheese (about 22g)
- **Snack:** a handful of almonds (about 6g)
- **Dinner:** salmon, new potatoes and green vegetables (about 32g)

After dinner, a [short walk](/blog/walking-after-meals/) is a good way to round off the day.

## The bottom line

Most adults need around 0.75g of protein per kg of body weight a day, and a little more as they get older or train regularly. Aim for a decent portion at each meal rather than loading up at dinner, and get it from a variety of foods.
